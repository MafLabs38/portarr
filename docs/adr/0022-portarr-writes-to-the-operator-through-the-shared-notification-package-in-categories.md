# 0022 — Portarr writes to the operator through the shared notification package, in categories

- **Status**: proposed
- **Date**: 2026-11-24 (reworked for categories and `MafLabs.Notifications` 0.2.0 on 2026-11-25)
- **Spec**: D-129; F-NOT-01 to F-NOT-04 (first slice); a caveat to D-16 and D-31

## Context

Portarr shows what needs attention (`IAttentionService`, one list for the MCP overview, the pages and, as its documentation foretold, notifications), but the operator is not on the page when a backup fails at night. The same need was solved in Mnemarr (its ADR 0042: an SMTP sender with STARTTLS, a password read from a file, a policy of one mail per episode with a few reminders and daily caps), and the sender was then extracted into a package that Mnemarr, Portarr and Orchestrarr share: `MafLabs.Notifications`. Portarr must not grow a second mail client, and must not become a mail cannon either: "tell the operator" is the only outbound message it will ever send.

A first version of this decision wrote one mail per problem (`area/kind`) with a plain-text template, and kept the policy (episodes, reminders, caps) in Portarr. Two things changed: the package (0.2.0) now carries the policy and an HTML mail layout, and the maintainer disliked a burst of separate mails and wanted to tune each area on its own (backups daily, maintenance off). This ADR records the result.

## Decision

1. **The sending, the rules of when to write and the look of a mail are the shared package's** (`MafLabs.Notifications` **0.2.0**, referenced by `Portarr.Core` through `Directory.Packages.props`): `INotificationSender` with an SMTP provider (STARTTLS on port 587, port 465 refused); `MailTemplate` and `MailLayout` (one definition rendered as **HTML and plain text**, every value cleaned and encoded: no control character, no markup, no header injected through a name); `NotificationCategory` and `CategoryPolicy` with their validator; `NotificationDecider` (when a mail is due, when an episode ends) and `NotificationPolicyOptions` (the delays and the overall cap, section `Notifications:Policy`). Portarr's own policy code that duplicated this (a `NotificationPolicy` class) is deleted. No new project: what remains is small, depends on the attention service, and lives in `Portarr.Core/Notifications` like the other monitors (ADR 0006); the controllers live in `Portarr.Api.V1/Notifications`.
2. **Notifications are segmented into categories, one per area of the attention findings**, each with its own template and its own settings: `apps`, `infrastructure`, `data` (backups) and `maintenance` (vulnerabilities, outdated images, CI, Dependabot, platforms out of support). A finding of another area (the catalog, the MCP servers) is not mailed, and neither is the Assistant: they send nothing. Per category the operator sets: **enabled**, the **minimum level** (`info`, `warning` or `danger`), the **reminder delay** in hours, the **maximum number of reminders** and the **maximum number of mails a day**. Overall they set the master switch, the recipient, the sender and the **overall maximum a day**. The category's id keys its settings and its log rows.
3. **Defaults** (declared by the application, in `PortarrNotificationCategories`; quiet on purpose, because little yet closes a problem once it is told: one mail, one reminder after 72 hours, at most 2 mails a day per category (maintenance: no reminder, at most 1 a day), overall 10 a day):

   | Category | Enabled | Minimum level |
   |---|---|---|
   | `apps` | yes | danger |
   | `infrastructure` | yes | danger |
   | `data` | yes | warning (a late backup is worth a mail) |
   | `maintenance` | **no** | danger |

   **Maintenance is off by default** because its findings are many and noisy (every repository without Dependabot, every image a patch behind) and nothing yet follows them until they are fixed: the reconciliation loop that would turn a finding into "fixed, closed" does not exist (the maintenance vertical's own follow-up). A mail that the operator learns to ignore costs more than no mail; whoever wants it turns it on, ideally at `danger`.
4. **One mail per category per pass.** The monitor reads the findings every minute, keeps the ones at or above their category's minimum level (none when the category is off) and keeps **one episode per category**: the first mail leaves when the category has had a qualifying finding for `ConfirmMinutes` (5); reminders follow per the category's policy while it lasts; the episode **ends** `ClearMinutes` (15) after the last qualifying finding, counted from the later of the last sight and the start of the run (so a restart whose tools have not answered yet does not end it and start a new one); a mail that fails is recorded, not counted as sent, and tried again after `RetryMinutes` (15); the two caps (the category's and the overall one) count the log, failed attempts and the test mail included, so they hold across a restart. The decision itself is the package's `NotificationDecider`. The mail groups every qualifying finding of the category, **one line per thing concerned** with its level and a sentence for its kind (a closed vocabulary: "backups are failing", "machines are not ready"…; an unknown kind is written as its code), and its level is the worst finding's. A burst of separate mails for one area is exactly what this avoids; two categories with findings at the same time are two mails.
5. **What is mailed is the text of Portarr and the names of the catalog.** Findings give closed codes, counts and names; never a message a remote tool sent (the one finding whose names are a tool's own words, `code-source-error`, is written as its sentence alone). The HTML is built by the package's layout (tables, inline styles, nothing loaded from outside, a dark palette for the clients that ask) with the product name "Portarr", a title per category ("Applications need attention", "Infrastructure needs attention", "Backups need attention", "Maintenance needs attention"), a call to action ("Open the Data page of Portarr to see the details") and a footer that says when the next reminder comes or that none will. There is also a test mail, HTML too.
6. **Off by default, one explicit recipient.** `Notifications:Enabled` is false; mail goes only to `Notifications:Recipient`, never to an address a tool, a catalog entry or a conversation gave.
7. **The password is a file**, `Notifications:Smtp:PasswordFile`, read by the package at each send (so it can be rotated), never an environment variable. The API returns **whether** a file is set and whether it can be read, never its content and not its path.
8. **The SMTP connection is a deployment setting, the rest is a setting of the page.** Host, port, user, password file and STARTTLS come from the configuration (`PORTARR__NOTIFICATIONS__SMTP__*`) and are read-only in the API: whoever could change the host while the password file stays would make Portarr hand the password to a server of their choice. The delays of the policy (`PORTARR__NOTIFICATIONS__POLICY__CONFIRMMINUTES`, `CLEARMINUTES`, `RETRYMINUTES`, `MAXPERDAY`) and the interval between two looks (`PORTARR__NOTIFICATIONS__PASSSECONDS`) are deployment settings too. What the page changes (`settings.manage`): the master switch, the recipient, the sender, the overall cap and the policy of each category. They are stored in the `Config` table (key `Notifications`: the global fields and a `categories` map from id to policy; JSON, no secret) and laid over the configuration and the defaults; the shared sender reads the settings in force at each send (the settings object is its `IOptions<NotificationOptions>`), so a change needs no restart. A change is validated before it is stored (the package's `CategoryPolicyValidator`, a 400 with one entry per field, `categories.data.reminderHours`…; an unknown category is refused). A stored policy that was corrupted by hand falls back to the category's default with a warning in the log, and never stops the monitor. Turning notifications on is refused while the recipient, the sender or the connection cannot work, with the package's own sentence.
9. **What was tried and what is going on is stored** (migration 004, never released, edited in place): `NotificationLog` (one row per attempt: kind `category` or `test`, the category id, the episode, the level, the reminder number, when, delivered, a short reason) pruned after 30 days, and `NotificationEpisode` (one row per category that is going on). A restart sends nothing twice, and the page can say why a mail did not leave.
10. **API** under `/api/v1/notifications` (all `settings.manage`): `GET settings` (with the categories, their title, description, current policy and defaults), `PUT settings`, `POST test` (counted in the overall cap), `GET log`.

## Consequences

- The operator learns of a failed backup or a down app without opening Portarr, in one readable mail per area, and tunes each area. Portarr still never acts on the infrastructure (D-16, D-31): it informs, by a channel the operator configured.
- A fix to the sender, the layout, the decider or the SMTP error sentences is made once, in the package, for the three applications.
- **A limit, on purpose:** the episode is the category's, not the finding's. When the set of findings changes while an episode lasts (a second asset whose backup fails, an app that recovers), **nothing extra is sent**: the next reminder lists the current set, and the pages show the change at once. The cost of a mail for every change is the burst the maintainer refused. If the category clears completely and comes back, it is a new episode and a new first mail. A category whose reminders are exhausted stays silent until it clears.
- Raising a category's minimum level, or turning it off, ends its episode like a recovery (after `ClearMinutes`, nothing is sent); lowering it starts a new one.
- The `SmtpClient` of the framework has no implicit TLS and is documented as not recommended for new code; for a rare mail to one person on port 587 that is acceptable (the same reasoning as Mnemarr's ADR 0042), and replacing it is a decision for the package, not for Portarr.
- The connection cannot be set from the UI: a deployment without environment access has to change its manifest. That is deliberate (point 8).
- A new finding kind needs one line in the sentence table (`NotificationTemplates`) to read well, and falls back to its code without it. A new category is one declaration (`PortarrNotificationCategories`) and one template; the quota of a service (the next vertical) will be one.
- Maintenance mails exist but start off; turning them on is the operator's choice until the maintenance findings can be closed.

## Alternatives considered

- **A mail client of Portarr's own**, or a copy of Mnemarr's. Rejected: three copies of the same security-sensitive code.
- **One mail per problem (area and kind), the first version of this ADR.** Rejected by the maintainer: a bad night for the infrastructure meant a burst of mails, and no way to silence one area.
- **One mail for everything.** Rejected: the areas have different urgency (a late backup is daily news, a vulnerable image is not) and the operator must be able to tune them apart.
- **A mail per finding, with no episode.** Rejected: a standing problem would write every minute, or the policy would have to live in the sender.
- **Keeping the policy in Portarr.** Rejected once the package carried it: the decider, the caps and the category validation are the same for the three applications.
- **A digest at a fixed hour** (F-NOT-03). Kept for later: a critical problem should not wait for the morning, and a digest is another policy over the same sender.
- **Editing the whole connection from the page.** Rejected for the reason of point 8.
- **The new `Notify` providers of §7.4 of the spec.** The channel is the package's `INotificationProvider`; Portarr has no reason to have a second abstraction over it.
